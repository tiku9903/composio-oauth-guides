How we check an app

Every guide answers one question: what stands between you and letting any of your users connect this app through your agent?

Why a visa

Three things get confused when you integrate an app through Composio:

  • The OAuth app is your agent's visa. You register it once with the provider. It holds a client ID, secret, redirect URI and allowed scopes, and its name is what users see on the consent screen. This is the thing that may need approval.
  • Your auth config in Composio says which OAuth app and scopes to use. Composio holds a visa for some apps already. For the rest you bring your own, registered with Composio's callback {{ REDIRECT_URI }}.
  • A connection is one user letting your agent in. Approval problems surface here: "unverified app" screens, 100-user caps, or a workspace admin who has to approve you.

Four kinds of entry

The entry type is judged for the scopes an agent realistically needs, not the easiest path. Gmail credentials take five minutes to create, but reading mail for the public needs a security assessment, so Gmail is "visa required".

"Tested live" can only cite a request the agent actually made. "From official source" must cite a page it read. A claim backed only by forums or blogs is "uncertain". Review times are usually anecdotal, and the guides say so.

What the agent does

  1. Identify the provider from a name or URL: official domains, developer console, a search plan.
  2. Research registration, scopes, review, partner programs and real-world timelines. Official docs first; pages about look-alike products are dropped.
  3. Read each page and keep only facts it states, each with a supporting phrase.
  4. Try it live: discovery metadata, the developer console, Composio's toolkit page, and the real authorize endpoint with a fake client ID and Composio's redirect URI.
  5. Write the guide from those facts and observations only. Drafts below the quality bar (too few steps, no review step for a visa-required app, wait times that contradict the entry type) are rejected and rewritten.
  6. Verify with a separate pass that checks each claim and records corrections, then code-level guards drop citations to pages the agent never read.

Where an agent stops

Signing in to a developer console, accepting terms, verifying a domain, paying for a security assessment, recording a demo video and signing partner agreements need a person with the account. Guides mark those steps "You do this" and hand you everything the agent could prepare.

Limits

  • Requests without your credentials can prove an endpoint exists and how it treats unknown apps, not what a reviewer will accept.
  • Some providers block automated requests. A blocked request counts as no evidence.
  • Rules change. Each guide shows when it was checked, and you can check again at any time.
